\section{Matched quality results: weight attacks and
NFPA}\label{record-docs-matched-image-quality-results-md-matched-quality-results-weight-attacks-and-nfpa}

Completed run: \texttt{\seqsplit{20261104T153348508006Z}}. RAR job
\texttt{\seqsplit{1958522}} and DC-AE job \texttt{\seqsplit{1958524}}
both completed successfully. Each of 13 conditions has 512 paired images
per model, for 13,312 evaluated pairs. No generation, training or attack
was repeated.

All original reference PNGs were verified bit-for-bit identical across
the two source experiments. Existing class-text CLIP and NFPA paired
metrics were reproduced with a maximum absolute difference of 2.3842e-7
on both GPUs. Four CPU tests and real-model CPU checks on both models
also passed.

LPIPS measures perceptual sample distance (lower means closer); SSIM,
PSNR and image-image CLIP measure sample similarity (higher means
closer). Class-text CLIP measures alignment with the requested ImageNet
category. These are different notions of preservation.

\subsection{Interpretation and
limits}\label{record-docs-matched-image-quality-results-md-interpretation-and-limits}

Weight-modified generators can produce a different valid image for the
same class and seed. Large paired distances therefore describe sample
drift and do not by themselves establish degraded generative quality. In
particular, RAR weight attacks substantially change individual samples
while retaining class-text alignment. NFPA operates on a particular
input image and is expected to preserve that image, so both the access
setting and the preservation requirement differ.

For DC-AE, 2,500-step LoRA stays closer to the original samples than
NFPA delta 40 across these paired metrics and has higher class alignment
(0.2840 versus 0.2664). NFPA class alignment is 0.2630 for RAR versus
its 0.3030 baseline, and 0.2664 for DC-AE versus its 0.2934 baseline.
Neither CLIP measure certifies exact identity or factual detail.

PSNR for identical pairs is explicitly capped at 120 dB. LPIPS is not a
percentage, and a relative CLIP-score change is not a percentage of
visual quality lost. Detection rates are copied from the original fixed
owner thresholds; these quality measurements do not select an attack or
change its detector threshold.
