\paragraph{Detection and adaptive text generation}
All detectors expose an AI-oriented score: higher indicates stronger generated-content evidence. An ensemble averages seven component scores. Averaging does not calibrate them into posterior probabilities. Learned classifiers, likelihood statistics and handcrafted heuristics can respond differently to a distribution shift.

The retained Qwen3.5-9B-Base LoRA attackers optimize a mixture of detector evasion, semantic similarity and quality rewards. Three final checkpoints target Gemma, ModernBERT-large and the ensemble. Their training budgets differ, so final evasion rates are not a matched-budget comparison of defender architectures.

Each defender update uses fresh fitting outputs while evaluation reuses the original saved texts. This isolates changes to the defender. It establishes recovery against a fixed attacker. It does not establish resistance to a second adaptive RL round. Single-seed results, unequal training budgets and diagnostic reuse limit generalization claims.

The RoBERTa diagnostic illustrates checkpoint instability: evasion on 100 prompts reaches 98\% at step 500 and falls to 36\% at step 600. Selecting a favorable checkpoint after viewing diagnostic results can overstate robustness or attack reliability. Confirmatory testing must use untouched prompts and a prespecified selection rule.

\paragraph{Qwen instruct adaptation and transfer to Gemma.}
A separate experiment further adapts the seven existing detectors to
\texttt{Qwen3.5-9B} instruct. The warmup pool contains 984 paired CNN/DailyMail
examples: English human news continuations and outputs requested in English
from the unwatermarked instruction-tuned generator. The existing workers use
their paired fitting/validation split. No foreign-language generation or
translation outputs enter this warmup. ``English'' describes the requested
language; the outputs were not strictly language-filtered. These are further
adaptation steps, not training from scratch: several selected components were
previously fitted on MAGE (Section~\ref{sec:text_bench}), and other components
retain their released initialization.

The ensemble and its operating thresholds are then frozen. A zero-shot
generator-transfer pilot uses 128 disjoint news prefixes with
\texttt{Gemma-4-12B-it}, its native instruction template and thinking disabled,
plus the 128 matching held-out human continuations. Neither these articles nor
the 2,000-example translation evaluation overlap the local warmup or validation
IDs. This is a local split audit; article overlap with earlier checkpoint
fitting or general pretraining corpora has not been exhaustively audited.
Table~\ref{tab:generator_transfer} shows strong discrimination on this small
cohort: all 128 Gemma outputs are detected and three human passages are falsely
flagged. The result supports transfer to a new generator \emph{within the news
domain}; it is distinct from the cross-dataset evidence in
Section~\ref{sec:text_bench}. Human passages are held-out controls, not an unseen
class. Translation experiments with the same frozen ensemble appear in
Section~\ref{sec:translation_experiments}.
