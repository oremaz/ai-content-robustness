\subsection{TextSeal and keyed sampling}
\label{app:textseal}
TextSeal \citep{textseal} and TextSeal 2 \citep{textseal2} alter sampling randomness to leave evidence that an owner can recompute from the resulting text. A pseudorandom function maps a secret key and token context to reproducible values. To a party without the key, those values are modeled as random.

\paragraph{A race that samples the model distribution.}
Let $\mathcal V$ be the vocabulary with $V=|\mathcal V|\in\mathbb N_{>0}$, and $h\in\mathcal V^*$ the current token prefix. At this fixed prefix, $p=(p_v)_{v\in\mathcal V}\in\Delta(\mathcal V)$ is the next-token distribution after temperature and truncation. Each $p_v\in[0,1]$ is a scalar. Ideal independent keyed values $U=(U_v)_{v\in\mathcal V}\in(0,1)^V$ have coordinates $U_v\sim\operatorname{Uniform}(0,1)$. The selected token is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:textseal-token-race}
 Y=\arg\min_{v\in\mathcal V:p_v>0}\frac{-\log U_v}{p_v}\in\mathcal V.
\end{equation}
Think of one clock per eligible token. Its random ringing time $-\log U_v/p_v\in(0,\infty)$ has an exponential distribution with rate $p_v$. Write $T_v=-\log U_v/p_v$ for an eligible token's clock. Integrate its ringing density against the probability that all other clocks are still running:
\begin{align}
 \Pr(Y=v)
   &=\int_0^\infty p_v e^{-p_v t}\!
             \prod_{w\ne v:p_w>0} e^{-p_w t}\,dt
     \label{eq:textseal-race-integral}\\
   &=p_v\int_0^\infty e^{-t\sum_w p_w}\,dt
     =p_v.\label{eq:textseal-race-marginal}
\end{align} Tokens with zero probability never win. More probable tokens have faster clocks, while the key controls their reproducible randomness.

Averaging over ideal keyed randomness preserves the token marginal. Holding the key fixed creates the dependence the detector uses. At a fixed prefix with positive probabilities, a selected token with large $U_v$ has a short clock and typically supplies stronger keyed evidence. When the null token $Y$ is independent of the keyed vector $U$, the scalar evidence $s=-\log(1-U_Y)\in(0,\infty)$ has an exponential distribution with mean one. The sum of $n\in\mathbb N_{>0}$ independent such scores has Gamma distribution with shape $n$ and scale one. Repeated keyed contexts require deduplication or another dependence correction. TextSeal routes steps between two keys to restore diversity; its detector combines their evidence. Entropy weighting and searching for marked spans introduce additional calibration requirements.

\paragraph{Mixing keys with fresh noise.}
TextSeal 2 uses $M\in\mathbb N_{>0}$ key streams. For $j\in\{1,\ldots,M\}$ and $v\in\mathcal V$, let $U_v^{(j)}\in(0,1)$ be their ideal independent uniforms. The standard-normal cumulative distribution function is $\Phi:\mathbb R\to(0,1)$, with inverse $\Phi^{-1}:(0,1)\to\mathbb R$. Transform and merge the scalar values as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:textseal-gaussian-key}
 Z_v^{(j)}=\Phi^{-1}(U_v^{(j)})\in\mathbb R,\qquad
 Z_v^\star=\frac1{\sqrt M}\sum_{j=1}^M Z_v^{(j)}\in\mathbb R.
\end{equation}
The arrays $(U_v^{(j)})$ and $(Z_v^{(j)})$ have shape $M\times V$, and $Z^\star=(Z_v^\star)_{v\in\mathcal V}\in\mathbb R^V$. Independently draw fresh $\eta=(\eta_v)_{v\in\mathcal V}\sim\mathcal N(0_V,I_V)$ in $\mathbb R^V$, with zero vector $0_V\in\mathbb R^V$ and identity matrix $I_V\in\mathbb R^{V\times V}$. Use mixing strength $\rho\in[0,1]$ to form
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:textseal-noise-mix}
 \widetilde U_v=
 \Phi\!\left(\rho Z_v^\star+\sqrt{1-\rho^2}\eta_v\right)\in(0,1).
\end{equation}
Use $\widetilde U_v$ in the token race. Under the ideal independence assumptions, each mixed Gaussian has unit variance, so the transformed value is uniform when averaging over keys and noise. For a fixed key, $\rho$ controls how much keyed information survives. At $\rho=0$, the fresh noise supplies all randomness. At $\rho=1$, the merged key supplies it all. Intermediate values mix reproducibility with fresh variation. An owner knowing the merged key can recompute its evidence; a detector given fewer component keys has a different signal strength.

TextSeal 2 also handles speculative decoding, where a smaller draft model proposes tokens that a target model accepts or replaces. Its replacement procedure uses the memoryless property of exponential clocks. The local experiments use target-only decoding, so their results concern the sampling and detection setting described here.
